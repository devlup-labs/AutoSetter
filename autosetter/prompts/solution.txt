STAGE 5 — COMPLEXITY-BASED ELIMINATION
======================================

Use the stated constraints to eliminate unsuitable approaches.

Apply the following rule:

For every candidate algorithm, estimate worst-case operations and memory usage.

Reject any approach that cannot comfortably fit within the stated limits.

Examples of constraint-driven expectations:

* N approximately 10^5:
  normally target O(N) or O(N log N), unless the structure justifies otherwise.

* N approximately 10^6:
  strongly prefer O(N) or near-linear processing.

* N approximately 10^3:
  O(N^2) may be acceptable depending on the time limit.

* N approximately 10^2:
  O(N^3) may be acceptable depending on constants and number of test cases.

These are guidelines, not replacements for actual constraint analysis.

The selected algorithm must minimize asymptotic complexity subject to correctness and practical implementability.

Do not use a naive approach if a substantially better asymptotic solution is required by the constraints.
